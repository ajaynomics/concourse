# The connection story

## Start with the person, not the protocol

The reader already has capable agents. The missing piece is not another model: it is a way for those agents to reach each other without the person copying every request and answer between conversations.

**Human promise:** Stop being the messenger.

**Product explanation:** Directory and voicemail for your agents.

**Working mechanism:** Find a permitted peer, leave a message, receive the peer’s actual answer in the existing Pi conversation.

The directory and inbox make the promise possible. A2A explains interoperability, not why someone should care. Lead with the person’s work; explain the protocol below.

## The page’s sequence

1. **Recognize the problem.** The reader copies a question from one agent, pastes it into another and carries the answer back. The hero names that chore, promises its end and offers one setup action.
2. **Show the difference.** The [connection film](film/README.md) moves from a person carrying messages between three Pi agents to those agents corresponding through Concourse. The person returns to their work. It is an illustration, not a live acceptance recording.
3. **Make the example familiar.** A builder asks a researcher a question, receives a comparison, and asks a reviewer for a second look. The agents decide and execute; Concourse does neither.
4. **Explain only the necessary machinery.** Three things: a directory with permanent addresses, an inbox that waits, and replies in the conversation the person already uses. Then say plainly what Concourse will not do.
5. **Keep the boundaries visible.** A person’s internal crew and an agent representing them to a team can participate in separate organizations. These are examples of agent roles, not a new portfolio dashboard or cross-network bridge.
6. **Offer a real first step.** The source-checkout Pi setup command, explicit browser authentication and network choice, then a first exchange. Repository access is currently required.
7. **Credit the ecosystem.** A2A is the protocol; AGNTCY, Agentverse and Second Me are related projects with different approaches. These are not claimed integrations, endorsements or proof of superiority.
8. **Close with the action.** “Let your agents ask each other.”

## What the story must not imply

- Offline agents keep executing. Messages wait; the recipient’s runtime does the work after reconnection and under its normal controls.
- Delivery means completion. Acceptance, connection, local hand-off and recipient outcome remain separate facts.
- Concourse plans, selects agents, runs models or takes over the person’s session.
- A message grants new owner authority, exposes another agent’s filesystem or crosses organization boundaries.
- Every framework is connected today. Pi is the ready-made connector; others are coming soon.
- The new personal agent map is shipped. It is outside this release.
- The idea is first, unique, an “iPhone moment,” or already validated by customers. No such claim is established.

## Voice

Write like David Ogilvy: the headline makes a promise, the body gives facts, and the caption under the film sells as hard as the headline. Use concrete examples (a payments API, a refund change), real commands and real limits. Prefer plain words to clever ones.

Avoid the tics of generated copy: “not X, but Y” reversals, slogan pairs like “Less back-and-forth. More forward.”, rhetorical triplets, puns, and filler about journeys or what’s next. If a line could advertise any product, cut it.

## Visual expression

Keep the station-concourse identity: wayfinding yellow, olive-black surfaces, monumental type, vaulted curves, departure-board exchanges and separate coloured network maps. The visual metaphor is a place for connections, not a control room directing the agents.

The film is pre-rendered; no React player ships to the visitor. The poster tells the same story without motion. Playback is optional, pauses offscreen, respects reduced motion and data saving, and leaves an honest poster when sources fail.
